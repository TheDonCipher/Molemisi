# MOLEMISI
# Field Journal — Narrative Voice (v1)

**Extends:** Bushveld Flora & Fauna v1, Inventory & Resource Specification v1.1, Bushveld Gameplay Simplified v2
**Changes to data model or API:** none. This is a content/style guide — it fills in the "optional collapsed lore line" that was already part of the design with an actual, consistent voice.

> Same caveat as every other document in this set: **Mogolo** (the name used below) is offered at moderate confidence as a term of respect for an elder — please correct if it's not the right choice.

---

## 1. The Voice

Every Discovery entry is written as if a village elder — **Mogolo** — is looking over the player's shoulder and naming what they've found. Deliberately **not** a fully fleshed-out character with a backstory, a face, or a name beyond the honorific: this is a voice, not an NPC. That keeps it light to write, easy to apply consistently across dozens of entries, and open to becoming a real character later if the game ever wants one without anything here needing to be rewritten.

Why an elder rather than a narrator or a dry field-guide voice: it gives every entry the same implicit relationship — someone experienced, a little amused, teaching a younger person what the bush is telling them — without having to establish that relationship separately in each entry.

## 2. Style Rules

- **One line, always.** The primary entry is a single sentence describing the physical sign — never the animal itself, matching the "signs, not live animals" rule from the Flora & Fauna doc.
- **One optional second line ("Mogolo's Note"), collapsed by default.** Carries the cultural/practical detail — never required reading, per the existing Progressive Disclosure rule.
- **Direct or implied address, not third-person reference-book phrasing.** "Mogolo says..." rather than "This species is known for..."
- **Reverence scales with rarity.** Common finds get a quick, almost dismissive line. Rare finds get a beat of stillness or seriousness in the writing — the voice itself signals how special something is, without needing a rarity badge to do that work.
- **Materials get entries too, on first collection** — shorter and more matter-of-fact than wildlife entries, since there's less to teach about a pile of wood than about an animal's passing.
- **Never explains the mechanic.** No entry should describe respawn timers, hotspots, or anything about how the game works — only what's in front of the player.

## 3. Entry Template

```text
[One-line sign description in Mogolo's voice]

Mogolo's Note (collapsed): [optional cultural/practical detail]
```

## 4. Worked Examples

### Open Bush

**Tholo (Kudu) — track, common**
> Deep, heart-shaped prints pressed into the sand. Mogolo says a kudu never hurries at dawn — it already knows where it's going.

**Phuduhudu (Steenbok) — track, common**
> Small, neat prints, close together. Mogolo calls the steenbok the smallest worry in the bush, and the easiest to miss entirely.

**Kolobe (Warthog) — track, uncommon**
> Blunt prints, dragged at the toe. Mogolo laughs every time — something so low to the ground has no business being that loud.

**Magogwe (Honey Badger) — dig, rare** *(term flagged TBD)*
> Torn earth and a raided nest. Mogolo goes quiet here — you do not want to meet the one who did this.

**Setlhare sa Phane (Mophane worms) — seasonal, rare**
> The branch is stripped bare, and something has been busy. Mogolo's face changes — this is the one they've been waiting all year to see again.

**Kgaka (Guinea Fowl) — feather, common**
> A speckled feather caught in the thorn grass. Mogolo says where you find one guinea fowl, six more are already watching you from somewhere close.

**Manong (Vulture) — feather, rare** *(term flagged TBD)*
> A long, dark feather drifts down from nowhere you can see. Mogolo tips his head back — something died near here, and something else already knows it.

### Riverbank

**Kwena (Crocodile) — track, rare**
> A wide groove drags down into the water and does not come back up the bank. Mogolo goes still, and so should you.

**Morula (Marula tree) — flora, uncommon**
> Yellow fruit litters the ground beneath the branches. Mogolo says even the elephants know when marula season starts before the people do.

**Letsopa (Clay) — material, first collection**
> Cool, heavy clay from the riverbank. Good for building. Better, Mogolo says, for teaching patience.

**Mokolwane (Palm Fiber) — material, first collection**
> Tough palm fronds, ready to be worked. The baskets sold at the Kgotla market all start exactly here.

**Catfish — ripple, common** *(Setswana name TBD)*
> The water folds over itself once, near the bank, and goes still again. Mogolo says a catfish sees you long before you ever see it.

**Kingfisher — dive, uncommon** *(Setswana name TBD)*
> A flash of blue drops into the water and comes up with something silver. Mogolo says blink at the wrong moment and you've missed the whole hunt.

**Otter — slide, rare** *(Setswana name TBD)*
> A smooth, wet groove runs down the bank, still glistening. Mogolo grins at this one — whatever made it was clearly enjoying itself.

**Waterbuck — track, uncommon** *(Setswana name TBD)*
> Broad, splayed prints sunk deep at the water's edge. Mogolo says the waterbuck never strays far from the river — it trusts nothing else to save it.

**Heron/Crane — print, uncommon** *(Setswana name TBD)*
> Thin, careful prints, spaced with patience. Mogolo says the heron holds still better than most men manage at anything.

### Rocky Outcrop

**Pela (Rock Hyrax) — track, common** *(term flagged TBD)*
> Small droppings tucked into a crack in the stone. Mogolo says the pela never goes far from home — sensible, for something so small.

**Tshwene (Baboon) — track, common**
> Scattered prints, in every direction at once. Mogolo shakes his head — a troop of baboons leaves a scene looking like an argument.

**Nkwe (Leopard) — track, rare** *(term flagged TBD)*
> A single clean print in the dust, and then nothing. Mogolo doesn't say anything at all — some things you're only meant to almost see.

**Matlapa (Stone) — material, first collection**
> Plain stone, but there's plenty of it. Mogolo says the rocks remember more than the trees do — they just don't talk about it.

**Quartz Shard — glint, rare (Discovery only, not a crafting material)**
> A piece of stone catches the light wrong — too clean, too bright for ordinary rock. Mogolo turns it over twice before he says anything at all.

**Raptor (kestrel/buzzard) — perch, uncommon** *(Setswana name TBD)*
> A shape circles once, high over the outcrop, and is gone. Mogolo doesn't bother looking up — he says you only see them once they've already decided you're not worth the trouble.

**Aloe — flora, common** *(Setswana name TBD)*
> A cluster of thick, spiked leaves wedged into a crack in the stone. Mogolo says it survives on less water than seems fair to anything else out here.

## 5. Totem Handling, Revisited

**Kwena's** entry above is a good example of where the totem hook (flagged as optional in the Flora & Fauna doc) can sit without overreaching: the line is written to feel weighty and specific *without* asserting a clan claim I'm not certain of. If you want to go further — naming which families carry Kwena, Tau, Tlou, or others as their own — that's a follow-up worth doing from what you already know rather than what a search turns up.

## 6. What This Doesn't Change

No new fields, tables, or endpoints. The "collapsed second line" mechanism already existed in the design — this document only decides *whose voice* fills it in and *how* to keep that voice consistent across everything still left to write.

## 7. Coverage

Every species and material from the Flora & Fauna doc now has an entry in this voice — nothing left to write from that list. Remaining open items across the whole project: folding Phane into the inventory spec, and the PixelLab asset list.
